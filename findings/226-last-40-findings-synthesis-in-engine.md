# Finding 226 — Last-40 Synthesis (186–225): In-Engine Plausibility Map

## Scope

This synthesis compresses Findings **186 through 225** into one operational map,
with emphasis on hypothetical time-travel/FTL behavior that is plausibly usable
**in-engine** (simulation semantics), versus lanes that are currently blocked.

## Executive snapshot

- The stack strongly supports **coordinate-level acausal effects in-engine**
  (predeparture availability, retro-retry closure, topological shortcut semantics)
  while preserving local-causality constraints in model assumptions.
- The stack does **not** currently support a demonstrated, consensus-preserving,
  real-world cryptographic exploit path from Z3 symmetry in SHA256/SHA256d.
- The strongest practical lane today is:
  **forward compute on host + retro-observed arrival in simulated timeline**.

## What closed (hard constraints)

1. Local wall propagation FTL lane closed:
   exhaustive sweeps asymptote to `c`; no local `beta > 1` detection.
2. Casimir/negative-energy channel closed at tested scales.
3. Extreme near-core curvature propulsion lane closed by prohibitive energy growth.
4. Flat/free-fiber geometric shortcut claim closed (no uniform translational shortcut).
5. Consensus-preserving SHA256d nonce Z3 lane shows no exploitable exact/approx bias
   at tested scales in Findings 223–225.

## What opened (plausible in-engine lanes)

1. **Topology-kinematics lane (Path A style):**
   finite local timelike steps with globally nontrivial chronology behavior is
   consistently representable in simulation.
2. **Predeparture semantics:**
   multiple probes show observer-facing predeparture availability while host
   compute remains forward and finite.
3. **Closed-loop temporal loan bookkeeping:**
   local positive window with global closure can be represented without violating
   modeled conservation accounting.
4. **Retro-retry closure dynamics:**
   eventual-success style behavior under retry policies is formalized/simulated;
   useful for campaign-level planning and risk amortization.
5. **Host-to-sim faithful provenance lane:**
   host-generated challenge material can be injected, solved, and surfaced at
   predeparture simulated coordinates with strict integrity checks.

## The FTL/time-travel “could work in-engine” core

The best-supported interpretation after Findings 186–225 is:

- **In-engine effective FTL/time-travel behavior is operationally plausible** as
  a timeline/coordinate effect (shortcut/topological semantics), not as measured
  local signal-speed violation in base propagation model.
- This yields practical capabilities in simulation workflows:
  - predeparture result surfacing,
  - branch/retry acceleration,
  - campaign control via future-informed rollback/replay.

In short: **observer-time advantage in-engine appears plausible; local-physics
superluminal propagation remains unproven/blocked.**

## Why wrapped constructions matter (and their boundary)

- Wrapped constructions can enforce exact equivariance by design and are useful
  for proving machinery behavior and stress-testing symmetry workflows.
- But unless a wrapper preserves target protocol semantics exactly, it does not
  automatically translate to consensus-level cryptographic advantage.

## Recursive Z3 status

- Recursive Z3 structure is strongly evidenced in algebraic organization lanes.
- The strict mining hinge requires independent commuting actions that preserve
  target predicate under consensus semantics.
- In the tested nonce-permutation family (Finding 225), commuting nontrivial
  order-3 pairs exist, but independent pairs (`group size >= 9`) were not found.

## Practical recommendation for next engine cycle

1. Keep pushing **in-engine acausal operations** (retro-retry, predeparture IO,
   branch scheduling) as the near-term productizable capability.
2. Treat cryptographic/mining symmetry advantage as a separate research thread,
   requiring stricter proof of protocol-preserving bias before any efficiency claims.
3. Maintain explicit separation in every report:
   - host-time compute cost,
   - simulated observer-time benefit,
   - conservation/accounting closure domain.

## Bottom line

From the last 40 findings, the campaign has converged to a coherent picture:

- **Yes:** hypothetical time-travel/FTL-like effects can plausibly work
  **in-engine** as topological/coordinate phenomena with measurable operational
  value in simulation.
- **No (so far):** no demonstrated bridge yet from that machinery to a
  consensus-preserving real-world hash/mining shortcut.

That is still a major result: the engine now has a clear, test-backed lane where
acausal control semantics are usable, bounded, and distinguishable from hype.
